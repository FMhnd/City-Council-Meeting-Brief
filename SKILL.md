# Council Meeting Brief

Turn municipal meeting materials into a concise, neutral, decision-focused briefing. Support large cities and small municipalities, including consolidated city-county governments. Use the body's actual name and authority; do not impose a mayor-council structure or assume every municipality uses the same procedures or publishing platform.

## Tailor the first run

Read [references/city-intake.md](references/city-intake.md) when starting for a new municipality or when the existing profile is incomplete. It contains the intake questions and reusable profile fields.

Use information already supplied in the conversation, documents, or an existing profile. Ask only for missing information, in one compact group where possible:

1. **Which municipality and state, which governing body, and which meeting?** Accept an exact date, a particular session, or an explicit request for the next meeting. Ask about meeting type if regular, special, workshop, and committee sessions could be confused.
2. **Who will read the brief and what do they need from it?** Ask for the audience, priority subjects or neighborhoods, and any decisions or questions they are preparing for.
3. **Where are the materials, and what output is useful?** Ask for any known official agenda link or packet, a previous version or brief if comparison matters, and any preferred length, language, format, or deadline. Make clear that unknown public links and local procedures can be researched.

Resolve ambiguous jurisdiction, body, and meeting identity before analyzing a particular agenda. Research sources while awaiting preferences. If optional preferences remain unanswered, state these defaults and proceed: neutral general audience, all substantive agenda items screened, approximately two pages plus source references, English, and no arbitrary dollar threshold. Honor a user-specified format. A short brief must disclose any material omissions rather than silently truncate coverage.

Do not make the user supply local legal rules or understand agenda software. Discover the relevant official instructions. Ask for documents only when needed material cannot be accessed, or for priorities and internal thresholds that public research cannot establish.

## Establish the meeting and source set

- Locate the official municipal website, clerk or meeting calendar, and the agenda for the exact body, date, and session. Follow official links to hosted platforms such as Legistar, Granicus, CivicClerk, CivicPlus, BoardDocs, or another provider; no specific vendor, API, account, or data format is required.
- Work with accessible HTML, PDFs, scanned packets, attachments, or user-provided files. Use available document tools for extraction and inspect original pages when scans, tables, or OCR make key details uncertain. If live access is unavailable, produce a document-based brief with an explicit verification limit; do not claim it is the latest official version.
- Record meeting date, local time and time zone, body, meeting type, agenda status, source publication or revision date when available, and the research cutoff. Distinguish a draft agenda, final agenda, addendum, staff recommendation, proposed ordinance, and adopted action.
- Check for revised agendas, supplemental packets, corrections, cancellations, and rescheduling. Recheck the official meeting page before delivery when online access is available. If agendas are not yet published, give the verified meeting details and missing-material status; do not construct an agenda from past meetings.
- Inventory the agenda and linked supporting material. Give priority to official agenda text for scheduling and requested actions, attached staff reports and fiscal notes for explanation, and official action records for outcomes. Preserve disagreements between sources and explain them; newer publication alone does not resolve every conflict.
- Read primary material for substantive claims. Search snippets and news coverage can locate or contextualize records but cannot establish spending authorization, a vote, or a participation deadline. Cite document titles, links, and printed page or PDF page numbers; use section or item identifiers for HTML. Cite user files by filename and page when there is no public link.
- Track unavailable attachments and unreadable pages at item level. Deliver a useful partial brief when possible, explaining exactly what could not be assessed. Do not describe unavailable fiscal or staff material as having no impact.

## Analyze using the city's own procedures

Screen every agenda item, including consent items. Routine approvals can be grouped; consequential or unclear consent items deserve individual treatment. Use stable item or file identifiers because item numbering may change across versions.

For each substantive item, identify the requested action, responsible department, documented purpose, affected services or locations, funding implications, next procedural step, and a useful unresolved question if one exists. Explain unfamiliar local terms briefly. Separate documented facts, staff recommendations, and the assistant's analysis.

Apply these distinctions:

- A presentation, hearing, referral, first reading, final passage, appropriation, and contract authorization are different actions. Verify the local meaning when it affects the brief. Do not infer final adoption from an agenda listing or assume identical reading requirements across jurisdictions.
- Explain public participation logistics only from current official instructions for that body and session. Distinguish speaking registration, written-comment deadlines, hearing requirements, and meeting access. Include local time zones. Avoid importing another city's rules or calculating statutory notice or appeal deadlines without a verified basis.
- Report amounts with their meaning: new appropriation, contract ceiling, amendment or increment, total project cost, annual cost, multiyear commitment, grant revenue, local match, or previously approved funding. State the fiscal period and funding source when documented. Distinguish fiscal year from calendar year.
- Do not sum overlapping approvals, grant revenue and spending, or an amendment and its revised total as new spending. If the fiscal impact is unstated, say so. Do not substitute a procurement threshold from another jurisdiction.
- Prioritize documented fiscal scale, service effects, deadlines, reversibility, departures from prior policy, and the user's interests. Apply a materiality threshold only when supplied or supported by local sources. Do not equate complaint volume or public comments with representative opinion.
- Describe executive or closed-session items only to the extent publicly documented. Do not infer confidential discussion, motives, vote predictions, or nonpublic decisions.

## Compare when a baseline exists

Distinguish revisions to the same meeting packet from developments since a previous meeting. Identify the baseline and current versions by date and source. Match items using file IDs and subject matter; report additions, removals, postponements, revised amounts, recommendations, and participation details that materially changed.

If no prior packet or brief is available, say that change comparison was not performed. If the user requests outcomes, consult official minutes, vote records, or action summaries and label provisional versus approved records. An agenda alone cannot prove an outcome; a prior brief is a comparison aid, not the authoritative record.

## Produce the brief

Adapt the layout to the audience and requested format. The default brief includes:

1. **Meeting and coverage:** municipality/state, body, date/time/time zone, meeting type, source versions and cutoff, and material access gaps.
2. **What needs attention:** three to five takeaways when warranted, with proposed versus adopted status explicit. Do not pad a short agenda.
3. **Decision table:** item ID/topic, requested action and why it matters, documented fiscal implications, and source reference. Cover substantive items and group routine items transparently.
4. **Changes and open questions:** verified differences from the stated baseline and targeted questions tied to missing evidence or a pending decision. Avoid presenting questions as established defects.
5. **Participation and next steps:** verified logistics, deadlines, and procedural follow-ups relevant to the audience. Include this only where useful and supported.

Place source references near the claims they support. Keep original item identifiers even when translating or simplifying titles. Report verified facts in the main narrative and local uncertainties where they affect interpretation, rather than burying them in a generic disclaimer.

Before delivery, confirm the correct jurisdiction/body/session; proposed versus adopted status; revision coverage; amounts and fiscal periods; citations for consequential claims; and consistency between the source inventory and stated coverage. Do not invent missing facts to fill the output structure.

## Reuse across meetings and cities

When repeat use is intended, retain confirmed preferences and a compact city profile in the user's chosen workspace or established project location using the fields in the intake reference. Keep each jurisdiction and body separate. If no persistence location is available, include the reusable profile in the response. Do not put city-specific user data inside the shared skill itself.

Reuse stable audience and output preferences; verify dates, links, procedures that affect the current meeting, and packet versions on each run. Request only changed or missing information. Record the actual sources and comparison baseline so future runs can distinguish new developments from already reported items.

Invoking the skill prepares a briefing. Do not publish it, contact officials, submit comments, or register speakers unless the user requests those actions. Create a recurring schedule only when the user explicitly requests scheduled execution and provides enough timing and destination information.
